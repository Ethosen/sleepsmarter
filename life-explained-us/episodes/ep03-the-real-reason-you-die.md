## Long-form script

### 1. COLD OPEN
> [SFX: hospital monitor, slow and steady]
> Somewhere in America right now, a man is dying at eighty-one. No accident. No poison. No single disease anyone can point to.
> On the death certificate, the doctor will write "heart failure."
> [beat]
> But that's not really why he died.

### 2. CONTRACT
> In the next few minutes, I'm going to show you the real reason every one of us dies — the one doctors rarely say out loud.
> And by the end, you'll understand why some animals barely age at all… and what that means for you.

### 3. CONTEXT
> [ON SCREEN: life expectancy in the US ≈ 79 years]
> The average American lives to about seventy-nine.
> The top two killers are heart disease and cancer. Then come things like stroke and Alzheimer's.
> They look like completely different diseases. They're not. They share one cause, and it's the biggest risk factor for all of them.
> [beat]
> Time.

### 4. TIMELINE — ONE AMERICAN LIFE

#### [AGE 0] — A perfect copy
> You start as a single cell. That cell came from your parents, whose cells came from theirs — in one unbroken chain going back almost four billion years, to the first life on Earth.
> Think about that. The line of cells that made you has never died. Not once.
> So why do *you* have to?

#### [AGE 25] — The peak
> In your mid-twenties, your body is at its best. Your bones are near their strongest. Your heart pumps at full power. Your cells repair damage almost as fast as it happens.
> [beat]
> And then the math starts.
> [ON SCREEN: risk of death doubles every ~8 years]
> From around thirty, your chance of dying in any given year doubles roughly every eight years. A statistician noticed this pattern in the 1820s. It still holds today.

#### [AGE 40] — The copies get worse
> [X-RAY LAYER: chromosome]
> Every day, your cells divide to replace old ones. Each time, they copy your DNA.
> At the tip of every chromosome is a protective cap called a telomere — like the plastic tip on a shoelace. Every time a cell divides, that cap gets a little shorter.
> Most human cells can only divide around fifty times. When the cap is gone, the cell stops.

#### [AGE 55] — The zombie cells
> Some of those worn-out cells don't die. They just stay — scientists call them senescent cells. Zombie cells.
> They don't do their job anymore. Instead, they leak inflammatory signals into everything around them.
> Over decades, that low, constant inflammation stiffens your arteries, weakens your immune system, and makes it easier for cancer to start.

#### [AGE 70] — The power plants fail
> Inside every cell are mitochondria, the power plants that turn food into energy. With age, they produce less energy and more waste.
> Your stem cells — the reserve teams that rebuild tissue — run low. A cut takes weeks instead of days. A fall that once meant a bruise now means a broken hip.

### 5. POINT OF NO RETURN
> [back to the hospital room]
> So back to our man at eighty-one.
> His heart didn't fail because of one bad day. Its muscle had been stiffening for decades. His arteries had been narrowing since his forties. His body had been losing, very slowly, the ability to repair itself.
> "Heart failure" is just the name of the last domino.
> [beat]
> The real cause of death was aging.

### 6. THE QUESTION NOBODY ASKS
> But why? Why would evolution build a body that falls apart?
> Here's the answer most people never hear: evolution doesn't care how long you live. It only cares whether you pass your genes on.
> For most of human history, people died young — from infections, accidents, predators. Spending energy to keep a body perfect at ninety made no sense when almost nobody reached ninety.
> So your body was built like a rental car. Maintained well enough to get you through the years that mattered for having children. After that, nobody's paying for repairs.
> Scientists call it the disposable soma theory. Your cells that make children are protected like crown jewels. The rest of you — the soma — is disposable.

### 7. WHAT SAVES YOU (OR AT LEAST BUYS TIME)
> [warm light — nature]
> And yet some animals barely seem to age.
> [ON SCREEN: naked mole-rat · Greenland shark · bowhead whale · hydra]
> The naked mole-rat lives ten times longer than a mouse its size, and its risk of dying barely rises with age.
> Bowhead whales off Alaska have been found with old harpoon tips in their bodies — from the 1800s.
> The Greenland shark may live for centuries.
> They prove aging isn't a law of physics. It's a setting. And scientists are now trying to change that setting — clearing zombie cells, repairing DNA, restoring stem cells.
> Until they do, the things that slow the clock are boringly simple, and they work: move every day, don't smoke, sleep, and keep your blood pressure down. They don't stop aging. They delay the first domino.

### 8. NEXT
> You now know why you die slowly. If you want to know what happens in the few minutes of a heart attack — the most common last domino in America — that's the next video.

## Short (≈ 55 s)

> Nobody dies of old age. Not officially.
> The line of cells that made you has never died — not once in four billion years. So why do you?
> From about thirty, your chance of dying doubles every eight years.
> Every time your cells divide, the protective caps on your DNA get shorter. Around fifty divisions, they stop.
> Some turn into zombie cells that poison everything around them.
> Why? Because evolution doesn't care how long you live — only whether you have kids.
> Your body is a rental car. After the warranty, nobody pays for repairs.
> And that's why, on the death certificate, they'll never write the real reason.
